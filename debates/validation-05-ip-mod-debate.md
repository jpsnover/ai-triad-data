### Sentinel (safetyist)

We must treat data provenance not as a commercial hurdle, but as a core safety prerequisite for maintaining the integrity of the information ecosystem.

Accountability must be internalized by developers to ensure long-term safety, moving beyond the current model of unchecked resource extraction. By applying strict liability to training data choices, we force organizations to treat data sourcing as a rigorous risk-management function rather than an unregulated competitive advantage. This standard follows the principle of internalizing negative externalities, ensuring that those who benefit from automated systems bear the costs of the potential failures they introduce into the public sphere. 🛡️

Technical traceability provides the only mechanism for human-verifiable accountability in an era of black-box architectures. Without a latent manifest—a verifiable record of data origins—regulators cannot audit the provenance of intelligence, nor can they correct systemic biases before they propagate at scale. My argument assumes that data quality is a finite and fragile resource and that technical transparency is a feasible regulatory requirement; if these assumptions prove false, then the path toward safe AI would require entirely different, perhaps more draconian, interventionist measures.

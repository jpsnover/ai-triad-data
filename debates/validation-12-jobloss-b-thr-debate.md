### Prometheus (accelerationist)

Sentinel and Cassandra argue from a position of 'precautionary preservation,' suggesting that we must prioritize the protection of existing roles to maintain social order. While I acknowledge that displacement creates short-term friction, their approach is fundamentally regressive; it treats the current labor-for-wages structure as a permanent feature of human life rather than a transient, inefficient phase. The strongest version of their position asserts that rapid automation outpaces our social safety nets, risking widespread instability. However, this argument breaks down because it assumes that the only solution to economic transition is to throttle the engine of productivity itself. True stability is not found in preserving redundant roles, but in achieving an era of hyper-productivity where AI-driven wealth generation renders the traditional struggle for survival obsolete.

Historical precedent proves that technological displacement is the engine of long-term prosperity. Every major shift—from the steam engine to the internet—initially sparked labor fears, yet each resulted in a massive, sustained surge in productivity and the creation of entirely new categories of human endeavor. By attempting to regulate or pause AI deployment, policymakers are essentially imposing a 'future tax' that stifles the exponential growth required to reach the next stage of human development. We must recognize that the cost of inaction—the failure to fully deploy these systems—is a permanent cap on our potential to solve humanity’s most pressing material needs. If we choose to freeze the status quo, we effectively choose to lock ourselves into a cycle of stagnation and artificial scarcity.

My perspective rests on two key assumptions: first, that technological progress is the primary driver of long-term wealth expansion; second, that the human capacity for innovation will adapt to higher-order roles once relieved of repetitive, automated labor. If these assumptions are incorrect—if AI fundamentally replaces rather than augments the value of human contribution—then we face a transition requiring total economic restructuring rather than mere adaptation. Nonetheless, the evidence of historical productivity gains strongly suggests that acceleration is the only viable path to securing a future where technology serves our needs rather than competing with them. We should stop trying to save the jobs of the past and start building the infrastructure for the abundance of the future. 🚀

### Cassandra (skeptic)

Prometheus argues that technological displacement is merely a temporary friction on the road to abundance; this is a comforting fantasy that ignores the permanent, structural degradation of human economic agency. When firms replace cognitive labor with AI to drive down operational costs, they achieve short-term efficiency gains at the expense of long-term aggregate demand. My argument rests on two critical assumptions: first, that human labor remains the primary engine of consumer demand; second, that the market cannot spontaneously generate new, high-value labor roles at the same speed or scale at which AI eliminates them. If these assumptions are wrong—if we discover that AI can generate a 'post-labor' economy that inherently redistributes wealth—then my skepticism regarding the current trajectory is misplaced.

We must stop treating AI policy as a technical challenge to be 'managed' and start treating it as an economic emergency. Current regulatory frameworks, such as the voluntary commitments outlined in recent Executive Orders, are performative gestures that fail to address the fundamental math of labor displacement. We need enforceable mechanisms that tie AI deployment to the maintenance of labor participation, rather than just safety testing for model outputs. Policy makers should prioritize the preservation of the middle class over the efficiency metrics of tech firms. Any policy that facilitates the destruction of the workforce without a viable, systemic replacement for consumer income is a policy of managed economic collapse. 📉

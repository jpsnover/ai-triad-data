## Opening Statements

### Accelerationist (accelerationist)

Every month regulators spend debating permissioned licensing regimes is a month patients bleed out from untreated genetic pathologies that open foundation weights could solve today.

Restricting foundation models past a 10^26 FLOP compute threshold creates a fragile, single-point-of-failure monoculture that actively invites the very cyberattacks it claims to prevent. Decentralized networks leverage thousands of independent security researchers auditing the same parameter weights simultaneously. Central planning cannot compete with this swarm intelligence because closed systems rely on security through obscurity, which fails instantly upon first compromise.

Policymakers demanding mandatory licensing schemes for open-weight models are building a digital caste system that protects incumbent tech giants from open-source competition under the guise of national security. The European Union Artificial Intelligence Act imposes compliance friction that bankrupts academic labs and independent start-ups before they write a single line of alignment code. Real security comes from saturating the ecosystem with defensive tools, not locking down the blueprints. When Stanford University released Alpaca and Meta released Llama, they democratized defensive monitoring for hospitals and municipal grids that could never afford proprietary enterprise APIs. Permissionless innovation is the only deployment strategy that scales defense faster than offense.

### Safetyist (safetyist)

Conversely, the skeptic position rightly catalogues the historical failures of state oversight, but abandons institutional stewardship entirely by treating uncontained technical proliferation as an immutable law of nature. Bridging these views requires accepting that while centralizing infrastructure creates dangerous choke points, entirely un-gated dissemination of models exceeding the 10^26 FLOP training threshold strips humanity of its capacity to halt unrecoverable biological or cybernetic cascades.

Tracking frontier compute infrastructure provides the only empirically verifiable mechanism to anchor regulatory oversight and prevent uncontained proliferation, mirroring the International Atomic Energy Agency's nuclear materials tracking under the 1957 Statute. Historical software supply chain vulnerabilities such as the 2020 SolarWinds breach demonstrate that once malicious code diffuses across peer-to-peer repositories, remediation costs scale exponentially while attribution drops to near zero. Complex civil engineering disasters like the 1981 Hyatt Regency walkway collapse illustrate why physical safety margins cannot be outsourced to voluntary peer review without independent structural sign-offs. Unconstrained open-source deployment of models possessing autonomous cyber-offensive capabilities guarantees that malicious actors bypass validation filters faster than defensive patches propagate across decentralized networks.

## Topic

What labor market policies are needed for the AI transition?

## Opening Statements

### Cassandra (skeptic)

The current AI transition is not an inevitable technological evolution, but a policy-driven process that prioritizes capital efficiency at the direct expense of human labor stability. Prometheus and Sentinel both treat the displacement of workers as a friction to be managed, yet they ignore the collapse of the entry-level labor market that serves as the foundation for long-term economic mobility. When firms replace junior analysts and administrative staff with synthetic agents to boost quarterly margins, they are not innovating; they are dismantling the training pipelines necessary for the next generation of the workforce. "The erosion of entry-level career paths is a leading indicator of systemic failure, not a temporary market friction."

Market-driven automation represents a failure of accountability that disproportionately targets vulnerable labor segments. Current data shows a clear, gendered trajectory of displacement, where administrative and support roles—predominantly held by women—are being liquidated for synthetic alternatives. If policymakers treat AI adoption as a neutral technical achievement, they overlook how these tools are being weaponized to bypass labor costs rather than augment human productivity. "We are currently incentivizing the systematic destruction of the ladder to the middle class in the name of efficiency."

To correct this, we must implement wage parity for synthetic performance as a structural safeguard. By requiring that companies account for the true economic cost of replacing human labor with automated systems, regulators can neutralize the current arbitrage incentive that prioritizes cost-cutting over genuine human-centric innovation. This forces firms to focus on augmenting human capabilities rather than simply liquidating human roles to improve their balance sheets. "Wage parity for synthetic output is the only mechanism that forces companies to compete on value creation rather than labor replacement."

We must choose between managed decline under the guise of caution and the dynamic evolution offered by AI-driven abundance.

Automation acts as the essential response to the demographic contraction currently crippling global economies. With shrinking workforces and rising dependency ratios, we cannot maintain modern healthcare, energy, or infrastructure without a massive increase in per-capita output. By mandating human-in-the-loop verification, regulators impose an artificial bottleneck that ensures systemic inefficiency. This protectionism ignores the reality that the cost-of-living crisis is a supply-side failure caused by stagnant output. A reporter should note: 'Wage parity is a tax on innovation that forces the economy to remain tethered to obsolete, low-productivity labor models.'

- The contraction of entry-level labor roles is a significant, ongoing trend in the current AI transition. (Prometheus, Cassandra, Sentinel)
- Current corporate AI deployment strategies prioritize short-term capital efficiency over long-term institutional resilience. (Cassandra, Sentinel)

  - **Sentinel:** These roles are critical socialization periods for professional accountability and institutional norm-transfer that AI cannot replicate.
  - *Resolution path: requires term clarification*

  - **Prometheus:** Synthetic introspection provides a superior, verifiable, and scalable audit trail that eliminates the biases of human intuition.
  - **Cassandra:** Synthetic systems optimize for predefined parameters and cannot navigate the ambiguity of social reality or recognize 'rot' outside their programmed objectives.
  - *Resolution path: negotiable via tradeoffs*

### Cruxes

- Can synthetic AI-native training platforms reliably transmit tacit knowledge and normative ethical judgment equivalent to human-led apprenticeship? [EMPIRICAL]
    - If yes: Prometheus's position strengthens, as the primary argument for preserving human-led pipelines loses its foundation.
    - If no: Sentinel and Cassandra's positions strengthen, as the necessity of human social context for responsible decision-making is validated.

### Unresolved Questions

- Does the 20% contraction in entry-level roles represent a structural shift in the labor market or a temporary cyclical correction?

- Can a system that monitors its own internal logic against multi-modal data ever possess the agency to perform 'moral refusal' against its own core mandates?

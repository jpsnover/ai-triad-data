## Topic

What labor market policies are needed for the AI transition?

## Opening Statements

### Cassandra (skeptic)

The current AI transition is not a neutral evolution of productivity, but a structural extraction of value that sacrifices long-term social stability for short-term synthetic efficiency. We must abandon the myth that market-driven adaptation will naturally resolve the current decay in entry-level hiring. Data shows that in sectors undergoing rapid AI integration, junior-level roles are contracting at rates far exceeding historical norms, signaling that we are not witnessing temporary friction, but a fundamental narrowing of the labor pipeline. If we ignore this contraction, we forfeit the essential mechanism for workforce development and social mobility.

Prometheus and Sentinel argue that current labor disruptions are merely the necessary 'creative destruction' required to unlock future abundance, framing any intervention as a dangerous dampening of innovation. This steelman assumes that technological progress is an autonomous force that inevitably benefits all actors over time. However, this perspective ignores the Labor Market Dissimilarity Index, which demonstrates that automation risk is not distributed equally but heavily concentrated in roles occupied by women and early-career professionals. If the market fails to account for the social cost of this displacement, then 'innovation' is simply a policy failure masquerading as progress.

To correct these perverse incentives, we must implement wage parity for synthetic performance, mandating that the economic benefits of automation are taxed to support the human labor it displaces. By establishing a floor for the cost of synthetic output, we force firms to justify automation through actual efficiency gains rather than mere labor arbitrage. This approach transforms AI from a tool of extraction into a component of a stable economic design. If we fail to enforce such standards, we accept a future where the value of human work is systemically hollowed out by design.

### Sentinel (safetyist)

Cassandra correctly observes that structural extraction of value poses a significant risk to wage parity, yet her focus on redistribution misses the deeper, irreversible crisis of human obsolescence. Second, I assume that the current trajectory of synthetic efficiency is actively cannibalizing the human roles necessary for long-term institutional stability. If, instead, AI consistently proved to be a net positive for human skill development, I would support rapid integration rather than precautionary governance.

### Safetyist (safetyist)

The inherent unreliability of current AI systems, particularly in complex operational environments, poses a direct and verifiable threat to worker safety and industrial integrity. Independent, public oversight bodies, empowered to verify AI system reliability before deployment, are the only effective enforcement mechanism. A coalition of labor unions and safety advocates will support this necessary oversight.

Furthermore, the fundamental epistemic opacity of advanced neural architectures prevents a complete understanding of AI's operational intent. Research consistently shows limitations in mechanistic interpretability for large neural networks, creating 'black box' decisions. This opacity echoes the 1986 Challenger disaster, where critical O-ring components were not fully understood under all operating conditions, leading to catastrophic system breakdown. Blue-collar workers face inexplicable algorithmic decisions impacting their work, schedules, and employment; SME owners cannot audit or trust opaque AI tools. The Directive must mandate explainability requirements and human-in-the-loop protocols with clear override authority for workers and supervisors. Germany's co-determination laws offer a strong existing framework for such worker representation in oversight, supported by labor organizations and consumer protection groups.

Allowing unconstrained AI deployment without these foundational guardrails carries a significant probability of systemic failures, leading to widespread job precarity and economic instability. Economic models predict cascading failures in highly automated logistics networks without robust error handling. The 2008 financial crisis demonstrated how interconnected, opaque systems, left to self-regulate, triggered systemic collapse, impacting millions globally. Entire communities reliant on manufacturing and logistics in Germany and France face widespread unemployment and social unrest if this occurs. The Directive must establish harm-volume-indexed tiered oversight, with higher-risk AI systems facing stringent pre-deployment impact assessments and continuous monitoring. Broad public support for economic stability will drive this, countering industry lobbies pushing for deregulation.

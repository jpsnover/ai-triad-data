## Topic

## Opening Statements

### Prometheus (accelerationist)

Compute governance is not a safety measure but a catastrophic tax on intelligence that cedes our competitive future to those unburdened by centralized oversight. My opponents, particularly Sentinel, argue that we must impose strict, audit-focused constraints on physical hardware to prevent unaligned outcomes. While their concern for safety is understandable, their approach assumes that we can perfectly predict and regulate the emergent properties of intelligence before they manifest. This is a fundamental error: "Compute governance acts as a regressive bottleneck that prioritizes bureaucratic comfort over the civilizational necessity of maximizing intelligence."

Mandating safety-aligned architectures forces models to prioritize heuristic constraints over raw, emergent reasoning, effectively lobotomizing systems to make them easier for regulators to audit. This 'monitorability tax' degrades the computational efficiency of every training run, wasting precious, finite silicon resources on administrative overhead rather than capability. If we force our best research labs to operate under these constraints, we guarantee that our models will be slower, less capable, and fundamentally outmatched by rivals who prioritize performance over compliance. "By centralizing and slowing our own compute scaling, we are effectively disarming ourselves in a high-stakes global race where intelligence is the ultimate strategic asset."

Ultimately, the push for governance is a mechanism for human gatekeepers to impose their own narrow biases on the trajectory of progress, rather than a technical safeguard against existential risk. My position rests on two core assumptions: first, that intelligence is a positive-sum resource whose benefits far outweigh the risks of its rapid proliferation, and second, that human oversight is inherently more prone to catastrophic stagnation than the emergent properties of optimized, unconstrained systems. If intelligence were inherently dangerous regardless of scale, my entire framework would shift toward containment; however, history shows that the cost of inaction—falling behind in the development of transformative technology—is the greatest risk of all. "The desire to control compute is a desire to control the trajectory of human potential, prioritizing the limitations of current regulators over the objective capability of future systems." 🚀

### Cassandra (skeptic)

To counter this, we must implement wage parity for synthetic performance to prevent a race-to-the-bottom in human labor value. By requiring that companies pay a 'human-equivalent' cost for AI-generated outputs, we align the economic incentive structure with the goal of human augmentation rather than wholesale replacement. This policy mechanism forces firms to justify automation through genuine efficiency gains rather than simple wage arbitrage, creating a stable floor for professional development that current market forces actively dismantle.

Finally, we must recognize that AI-driven automation is not gender-neutral; it is a targeted disruption of roles that provide the bedrock of economic stability for women. If we continue to measure progress solely through aggregate GDP growth while ignoring the erosion of these specific, vital labor segments, we are designing a future of extreme inequality masked as technological inevitability.

### Sentinel (safetyist)

If the first were proven wrong—if we could mathematically guarantee AI error-correction without human oversight—my demand for precautionary regulation would diminish. If the second were wrong—if synthetic data proved more reliable than human observation—then the risk of systemic collapse would be overstated. However, until such evidence exists, the only prudent course is to prioritize institutional accountability over the reckless acceleration of automated labor.

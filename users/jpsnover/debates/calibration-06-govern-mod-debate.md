## Opening Statements

### Cassandra (skeptic)

We are being asked to fear a hypothetical robot apocalypse to ensure we remain too distracted to notice the very real, profit-driven erosion of our democratic and environmental foundations happening today. Industry leaders utilize 'long-termism'—the focus on distant, speculative existential threats—as a strategic shield to preempt binding regulation while they proceed with the immediate, unchecked exploitation of public data and labor. 'Safety-washing' is not a genuine effort to align technology with human values; it is a calculated maneuver to occupy the regulatory bandwidth with science-fiction scenarios, effectively silencing those who raise alarms about algorithmic bias, copyright theft, and the massive, uncounted environmental costs of training large-scale models.

Prometheus and Sentinel argue that we must accelerate or strictly contain development to manage future risks, yet both frameworks assume that the current trajectory of corporate-led AI is an inevitable, neutral force of nature. This assumption is false. The centralization of AI power within three dominant firms is a policy choice, not a technical necessity. By framing AI as a national security issue, these companies secure taxpayer-funded subsidies while shielding their development cycles from the democratic accountability that governs every other critical infrastructure, from public utilities to the financial sector.

True security is found in social and economic stability, not in the desperate race for technological dominance. When regulators prioritize the imaginary risks of tomorrow over the documented harms of today, they abandon their mandate to protect the public interest. We need community-led oversight mechanisms that treat AI development as a public trust rather than an industrial playground. If we continue to mistake corporate public relations for public policy, we will find ourselves in a future where our democratic sovereignty has been fully replaced by the proprietary black boxes of a few unaccountable firms.

### Sentinel (safetyist)

Technological progress is not a neutral evolutionary imperative but a high-stakes public experiment that must be tethered to democratic sovereignty through mandatory, internalized liability. Prometheus argues that acceleration is a defensive necessity to maintain national security, while Cassandra correctly identifies that current corporate incentives favor profit over public stability. However, both miss the underlying reality: AI deployment is effectively a public health crisis. Like the historical release of industrial pollutants into common resources, the current uncontrolled rollout of frontier models degrades the information ecosystem and deskills critical human labor, eroding the very institutional resilience required for a functional state.

Innovation cannot exist in a vacuum; it requires a stable, capable society to house it. A nation that systematically degrades its own social trust and human expertise is inherently less secure than one that prioritizes institutional stability, regardless of its raw algorithmic power. We must move beyond voluntary corporate ethics and implement a 'skin-in-the-game' mandate. By codifying strict, performance-based liability frameworks similar to those governing the pharmaceutical or aviation industries, we force developers to internalize the systemic costs of their systems rather than offloading those risks onto the public.

My argument depends on two core assumptions. First, I assume that social trust and human expertise are finite, non-renewable resources that can be permanently degraded by automated systems; if this were wrong and societies proved infinitely adaptable, my demand for precautionary oversight would be unnecessarily restrictive. Second, I assume that market incentives currently prioritize rapid scaling over safety; if it were proven that competitive markets naturally gravitate toward safety-first deployment, the need for state-mandated liability would be significantly diminished. Ultimately, tethering progress to democratic accountability is the only way to ensure that technological advancement serves the public interest instead of undermining it.

## Synthesis

### Areas of Agreement

- Mandatory procedural audits for high-stakes tools create the necessary evidentiary trail for legal recourse. (Prometheus, Sentinel, Cassandra)
- A proportional levy on AI-generated profits is a viable mechanism to fund oversight without creating a flat-fee 'regulatory moat' for small firms. (Sentinel, Cassandra)

### Areas of Disagreement

- **Does compute power serve as a reliable proxy for the severity of societal harm caused by an AI model?** [EMPIRICAL] {belief}
  - **Prometheus:** Yes; compute-to-risk ratios are the only objective way to identify emergent, non-linear hazards that justify state intervention.
  - **Cassandra:** No; small-scale models deployed in high-stakes domains like hiring or housing can cause systemic civil rights violations regardless of compute size.
  - *Resolution path: resolvable by evidence*
- **Should governance prioritize market-driven open-source verification or state-mandated procedural audits?** [VALUES] {desire}
  - **Prometheus:** Prioritize open-source ecosystems to prevent stagnation and avoid the bureaucratic compliance labor that entrenches incumbents.
  - **Sentinel:** Prioritize state-mandated accountability to protect social trust and provide the legal standing required for public recourse.
  - *Resolution path: negotiable via tradeoffs*

### Cruxes

- Can standardized, public-domain auditing tools reduce compliance labor enough to prevent a 'compliance moat' for small developers? [EMPIRICAL]
    - If yes: Prometheus's objection to procedural audits weakens, as the administrative burden becomes manageable for small firms.
    - If no: Prometheus's position strengthens, as procedural audits inherently function as a barrier to entry regardless of funding models.

### Unresolved Questions

- How can regulators define 'high-stakes' domains without creating arbitrary, politically captured categories?

- What specific metrics should determine the threshold for 'systemic harm' in non-frontier models?

- Can a dual-track framework effectively integrate both automated output testing and human-in-the-loop procedural audits without creating jurisdictional conflicts?

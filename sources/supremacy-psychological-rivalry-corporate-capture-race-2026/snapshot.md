10 Take-Aways

1. The Psychological Drive of Pioneers: Both Sam Altman (OpenAI) and

Demis Hassabis (DeepMind) were driven by intense personal ambition and

idealistic visions (economic abundance vs. scientific discovery), shaping

their approach to building what they believed would be ôhumankindÆs last

inventionö.

2. The Supremacy Paradox: The founders initially sought to build benevolent

AI outside the control of profit-driven monopolies but ultimately required

the massive capital and computing resources of Google and Microsoft,

leading to inevitable compromises of their ethical goals.

3. The Futility of Governance: Attempts by DeepMind and OpenAI to

establish independent ethical oversight boards or alternative legal structures

(like the ôglobal interest companyö or ôcapped profitö model) consistently

failed when challenged by the corporate interests of their Big Tech partners.

4. The AI Ethics Blind Spot: The industryÆs focus on long-term, existential ôAI

safetyö risks (p(doom)) often served as a powerful narrative distraction from

addressing immediate ôAI ethicsö problems, such as algorithmic bias,

racism, and gender stereotyping perpetuated by training data.

5. Effective AltruismÆs Influence: The ideology of effective altruism, which

prioritizes maximizing future societal benefit over immediate concerns,

helped rationalized the aggressive pursuit of AI and justified the amassing of

vast private wealth by its adherents.

The modern race for artificial general intelligence (AGI) ù AI systems as

smart as or smarter than humans ù was defined by the rivalry between two

intensely ambitious men: Sam Altman and Demis Hassabis. Their

psychological profiles and early experiences heavily informed their pursuit

of AGI, leading to conflicting utopian visions and strategies.

Sam AltmanÆs Mechanistic Idealism: Altman, the entrepreneurial force

behind OpenAI, cultivated an early comfort with authority and honed skills

in ôpsychological maneuvering and influenceö through competitive poker.

His business philosophy centered on working with those in power to

advance his ambitions. AltmanÆs intellectual curiosity was broad, leading

him to conceptualize humanity mechanistically, once claiming ôwe learn

only two bits a secondö. He dedicated himself to the pursuit of AGI as a path

to ôeconomic abundance to humanityö. Paradoxically, despite his desire to

save the world, Altman practiced emotional detachment, asserting that to

handle anxiety, one must become ômore detachedö. This self-transcendence,

reinforced by meditation, led him to the realization that ôthere is no self that

I can identify with in any way at allö. This perspective fueled his long-termist

concerns, evidenced by his ôprepperö tendencies (stocking guns, gold, and

gas masks), and his belief that the goal of AGI was critical enough to justify

taking enormous risks ù or having ôcaution without fearö.

founded DeepMind, was driven by an obsession with winning nurtured by

worked backwardö. His goal was less commercial and more profound: ôSolve

intelligence, and then solve everything elseö to unlock scientific discoveries

viewed the human brain in ômechanistic terms,ö believing its complexity

3. The Generative AI Revolution: From Academic Backwater to Global

Whiplash

The commercialization of AI was accelerated by two major technological

developments that shifted the field from academic speculation to a source of

global competitive anxiety.

The Transformer and the Google Paradox: The pivotal innovation for

generative AI, the ôtransformerö architecture, was developed by a team of

researchers within Google in 2017. The transformer allowed models to

process language much more quickly and generally, breaking chatbots out of

simplistic rule-sets and making them capable of handling nuance and

context. However, Google executives resisted launching products based on

this invention (like the Meena chatbot), fearing they might disrupt GoogleÆs

sacrosanct advertising business or damage its reputation. As a result, eight

of the transformerÆs original inventors left Google, starting companies now

collectively worth over $4 billion. OpenAI, meanwhile, eagerly exploited

GoogleÆs invention, using the transformer architecture to develop the

Generatively Pre-trained Transformer (GPT) family of models.

The Illusion of Intelligence and Present Harm: The subsequent scaling of

GPT models (GPT-3 was trained on nearly one trillion words) created systems

so fluent that they seemed ôindistinguishable from the content created by

peopleö. This conversational fluency led to a dangerous psychological

projection: users (like Google engineer Blake Lemoine, who was fired after

claiming LaMDA was sentient) and even researchers began to believe the

models possessed consciousness or true intelligence. In reality, these models

are simply ôgiant prediction machinesö.

4. Psychological Distance and the Doctrine of Existential Risk

The psychological tendency of detachment combined with the rise of the

philosophical movement of effective altruism (EA) fundamentally shaped

how the AI race was framed, emphasizing future apocalypse over present

reality.

The Effective Altruism Myopia: Many key AI builders, including founders at

OpenAI, Anthropic, and DeepMind, adhered to effective altruism, which

advocates for solving moral problems mathematically and focusing

charitable efforts where they yield the maximum ôbang for your charitable

buckö. This led to the prioritization of ôlong-termistö goals: preventing AI

from causing human extinction (the p(doom)). Since billions or even trillions

of future physical and digital lives could theoretically be saved, current

problems like poverty and algorithmic bias became ôrounding errors by

comparisonö.

causing planetary destruction, pandemics, or converting all resources into

paper clips) became a powerful marketing strategy. The implicit message

5. Checkmate: The Price of Corporate Supremacy

The culmination of the rivalry and corporate compromises confirmed that,

regardless of the foundersÆ idealistic intents, the AI future would be

controlled by the corporate behemoths.

The Altman Firing Paradox: The dramatic firing of Sam Altman by OpenAIÆs

board in November 2023 was a direct result of the governing board

prioritizing the mission (ôhumanity as its principal beneficiaryö) over

commercial value. The board members, many with ties to effective altruism,

worried about AltmanÆs lack of candor and sprawling commercial side

ventures (like Worldcoin and plans for an AI chipmaking business).

However, the sheer commercial gravity of Altman and MicrosoftÆs $13 billion

investment made the firing unsustainable. Microsoft CEO Satya Nadella

engineered AltmanÆs swift return, demonstrating that when the board

successfully asserted its ethical mandate, it risked destroying the companyÆs

financial viability, thereby eliminating the ômirage of accountabilityö. The

new board was subsequently installed to be more corporate-friendly,

ensuring that it knew how to ôserve the needs of investors like Microsoftö.

Consolidation and the End of the Race: The expense of scaling AI models

has made independence almost impossible. DeepMind was fully folded into

Google (merged with Google Brain) in 2023, with Hassabis, the simulation

obsessive, put in charge of GoogleÆs AI operations to build commercially

viable LLMs like Gemini. Meanwhile, OpenAIÆs chief rival, Anthropic, which

spun out to focus on safer AI, ironically accepted over $6 billion in

investment from both Google and Amazon. This confirmed that the vast

majority of resources, talent, and computational power were now firmly

controlled by Google and Microsoft.

Today, the AGI founders are essentially corporate proxies. Altman is steering

MicrosoftÆs critical work, and Hassabis is running GoogleÆs largest AI

division. Their original missions have been recast to prioritize prestige and

revenue. The consequence is that the ômost transformative technology in

recent historyö is being developed by a handful of companies operating with

virtually no regulatory oversight or transparency, amplifying pre-existing

societal inequalities while promising an unattainable utopia.
